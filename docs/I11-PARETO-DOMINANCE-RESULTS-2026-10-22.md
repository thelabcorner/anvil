## 1. The dominance criterion, evaluated against the stored I20 evidence

The criterion (prereg §1.1) asks the canonical route, at a named preregistered profile, to be
**strictly smaller in bytes than every point** of a dense front, **at least as fast to decode as
every point**, **at most** every point's peak decode RSS, **at least as fast to encode**, and
**no larger in decoder-only binary size**.

The evaluation below uses **already-stored measurements** — the I20 closure runs (artefacts in
`tests/i20-evidence/`) and the I20 dense-grid control (`docs/I20-RESULTS-W6-dense-reference.md`,
runs `39660612517` / `39669395005`). **No new compute was spent to produce it.** It is labelled
`DERIVED` throughout.

### 1.1 Silesia — the candidate is the byte champion and loses the speed and memory axes

Candidate: the I20 closed profile `--ratio-bwt-min-gain-pct 3` → **48,281,716 B**, decode
**1.267–1.617 s** → **131.1–167.3 MB/s**, peak decode RSS **77,888–80,140 KiB = 76.1–78.3 MiB**
(runs `39696090369`, `39697830840`, `39697833411`, `39697836346`, `39697838963`, `39697841540`).

Reference front (Silesia, same-job dense grid, `docs/I20-RESULTS-W6-dense-reference.md`):

|---|---|---|---|
| complete compressed bytes | **48,281,716** | the smallest reference in the front is `xz -9e` at **48,456,004** (Brotli q11/lw30 is 49,383,136) | **PASS** — the candidate is the **smallest** thing in the 21-point front |
| whole-corpus decode | 131.1–167.3 MB/s | `zstd-9` **533.372 MB/s**, `zstd-1` **589.552**, `zstd-22` **472.953**, `lz4-9` **433.473**, `brotli q11/lw22` **279.759** | **FAIL** — short by **3.2×–4.5×** |

**So the dominance criterion is currently falsified on two of its five axes and not evaluable on
two more.** Only the byte axis passes — and it passes decisively.

### 1.2 The finding that redirects the programme

> **The binding competitors on decode speed and on memory are the zstd tiers and lz4, not Brotli.**
> Brotli q11/lw30 is the **byte** champion (49,383,136 B) and `xz -9e` is second (48,456,004 B);
> but the fastest decoders in the front are `zstd-9` (533.372 MB/s), `zstd-1` (589.552 MB/s),
> `zstd-22` (472.953 MB/s) and `lz4-9` (433.473 MB/s), and the smallest-memory decoders are
> `zstd-9` (8.5 MiB), `lz4-9` (9.6 MiB) and `zstd-19` (12.3 MiB) — all of them **larger** in bytes
> than the candidate.

**This has a direct architectural consequence, and it is the central finding of I11 so far.** The
canonical ratio route's speed and memory are owned by its **BWT backend**, which decodes at
**≈32 MB/s** because the inverse BWT performs `n/2` dependent random accesses into a `4n`-byte LF
array (source-verified: `third_party/libsais/src/libsais.c:7854-7892`). **No encoder-policy knob can
change that count** — the I20 walk-budget sweep measured the fitted Amdahl share at
`f = 0.000 ± 0.02` against the ≈0.70 that would be needed. **A BWT-backed route therefore cannot be
zstd-class on decode speed or on memory, no matter how the byte budget is spent.** Reaching
dominance over a front that contains the zstd tiers requires a route whose decoder is *not*
BWT-bound — i.e. ANVIL's **token-mode routes** (mode 10 rANS, mode 11 sparse-corrected, mode 12
SHAPE, mode 15 HOTOP), which decode from a small working set at a few hundred MB/s.

**This makes candidate D6 (the local Class-A grid) and a token-mode dominance arm the two paths
that can actually satisfy the criterion, while D1–D3 bound how much the BWT route can ever give.**

### 1.3 What the encode axis is likely to show, and why it matters to the criterion

`DERIVED` expectation, to be **measured** by candidate D5 (not asserted here). The canonical route
encodes Silesia by routing 193.5 MB through **Brotli q11** (quality 11 is the slowest Brotli
setting by construction) and 18.4 MB through the BWT. The front's fast encoders are `zstd-9`,
`zstd-19`, `zstd-22` and `lz4-9`. **A q11-based max-ratio route is therefore expected to be
roughly an order of magnitude slower to encode than the zstd tiers**, and the dominance
criterion's encode axis ("at least as fast to encode as every reference point") is **structurally
unlikely to be reachable for any max-ratio q11 route**, independent of any mechanism work.

**This is the second strategic finding of I11, and it is the one that should shape the programme's
next decision:**

> **The dominance criterion, taken against a front that contains the zstd tiers on *all five*
> axes, is not a gate that a max-ratio q11 route can pass** — it demands simultaneously the
> smallest bytes (Brotli-class) and the fastest encode/decode and the smallest working set
> (zstd/lz4-class). Those are different codec classes. The I20 closure is the **achievable**
> frontier position for this route (bytes + decode + peak RSS against the route's own two
> **binding** references, `xz -9e` and Brotli q11/lgwin30 — the references the repository's own
> gate names), and it is closed and reproducible. A genuine "overarching dominance" claim, in the
> strict sense of §1.1, would require either a **fast-mode profile** of ANVIL (a second codec
> class) or a **restatement of the criterion's front** by the repository owner.

**This is a statement about the criterion, not a relaxation of it.** No threshold has been changed,
and the prereg's decision rules stand as written; the finding is reported so that the programme's
spend goes to the axes that can still move.

### 1.4 enwik8 — the same structure, one level worse

The enwik8 dense grid (`docs/I20-RESULTS-W6-dense-reference.md`) has the same shape: the candidate
(23,537,422 B) is the byte champion, below `xz -9e` (24,831,648 B) and `brotli q11/lw30`
(24,810,180 B); the fastest decoders are again the zstd tiers. And on enwik8 the **candidate's
decoder is BWT-bound over the whole 100,000,000 B file**, so its decode is `≈3.30× xz -9e` and
`≈4.60×` Brotli and its peak RSS is `7.2719× xz -9e` (run `39696093027`, job
`gate-closure (enwik8)`, artifact `anvil-i20-gate-closure-enwik8-39696093027`, ruling
`FRONT-GAP_COST`) — **enwik8 remains a measured NO-GO**, and §1.2 says why: the BWT backend is the
whole file there, so the byte advantage and the speed advantage are mutually exclusive on that
corpus.
